## Before

AY asked Codex to derive a client-facing service system from the transcript because he had no capacity to do the product and content reasoning himself. The real user was the prospective client, not AY reading an internal analysis.

## Failure Mechanism

Codex still made AY act as product manager and editor. He had to repeatedly correct service taxonomy, package inclusion, price semantics, course/project overlap, recipient pressure, mobile density, visual direction, and interaction behavior.

The low-energy signal changed tone but did not change ownership of the reasoning. That is the core failure.

## Better Route

1. Treat the capacity signal as a collaboration constraint: minimize questions and take ownership of synthesis, comparison, drafting, and self-review.
2. Mine the supplied source for audience, jobs, fixed facts, offer boundaries, dependencies, and unknowns; ask only for a blocker that cannot be safely inferred.
3. Freeze a private service model before production: option taxonomy, inclusions, overlap, price semantics, user choice, and what happens next.
4. Produce the real recipient-facing artifact, then review it as the recipient: Can I understand the choices, differences, price, and next step without anxiety?
5. Inspect mobile reading and core interaction. Resolve internal inconsistency yourself before asking AY to look.
6. Mark unsupported price, promise, or scope as unknown. Low energy is not permission to invent commitments or perform unrequested external actions.

## Scope And Counterexample

Apply only when low capacity is explicit or unmistakable and the task can progress safely from supplied material. Do not silently choose an irreversible business commitment, payment, publication, or external message. If a missing fact materially changes those actions, ask one narrow question.
