# Audience-Facing Reports

Use this reference for public analysis, user research, product or market reports, strategy reports, and other report-shaped artifacts written for a reader beyond the working team.

## Reader Contract

Decide before drafting:

| Question | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Reader | Name the real reader or role, not “everyone.” |
| Decision need | State which belief, decision, or next action the report must change. |
| Evidence boundary | Separate external evidence, user/interview evidence, internal data, and derived implications. |
| Exclusions | Remove raw process, internal scaffolding, and material that does not change judgment. |

## Editorial Point Of View

A report is not a container for everything discovered. Decide the narrative spine before arranging sections:

1. what the reader needs to understand now;
2. the few conclusions that change that understanding;
3. the mechanism connecting those conclusions;
4. the implication, choice, or next question that follows.

Prioritize conclusions by decision value, not by research chronology or a generic “background-method-findings-recommendations” template. Merge findings that support the same judgment; separate findings only when they lead to different implications. Background earns space only when the reader cannot understand or trust the conclusion without it.

## Framework-Only Deliverable

When AY asks to align on the report framework before research or filling content, design the reader's decision path without writing a miniature report. Use one compact architecture table, normally no more than seven rows, with:

`reader question / conclusion slot to earn / evidence or comparison needed / output form / exclusion`

Then add one shared comparison/evidence matrix, normally no more than ten rows, and at most three scope choices that must be confirmed. End there. The architecture should make the final report conclusion-first, show how claims will be proved, and expose the reading order at a glance. Classification, user demand, capabilities, single-product analysis, evidence quality, and implications are rows or fields in these two tables, not standalone visible chapters. Do not add definitions, subquestions, product-card templates, research protocols, sample outputs, execution instructions, or a second version of the same taxonomy.

For a CEO-level competitive report, keep the visible framework centered on the market judgment, current versus emerging demand, product routes, capability mechanism and evidence, price or adoption cost, strengths and boundaries, and decision implications. Keep research operations and source administration outside the main reading path. Do not write “for the CEO” or other internal audience notes into the reader-facing report.

## Conclusion-Evidence Rhythm

Lead each major section with the conclusion that answers the research question. Follow it with the smallest evidence set that earns the claim, then state the implication.

```markdown
**结论：...**

- Evidence or user signal.
- Counterevidence or boundary.
- Product or decision implication.
```

Do not make the reader infer the point from a pile of interviews, tables, or background sections. Do not turn “why this report exists” into the first conclusion; conclusions answer the research question.

## Functional Density

Every paragraph must perform at least one job:

- decision or conclusion;
- evidence;
- implication;
- risk or boundary;
- next action.

Cut anything that only creates surface completeness, explains the obvious, repeats an earlier point in softer words, or exposes the internal work process.

When AY explicitly asks for only conclusions and appendix, use exactly that shape. Do not preserve background, purpose, and methodology as separate sections unless they change a conclusion or the reader's trust in the evidence.

## User Voice

For interview-backed reports, use short user voice snippets when they carry demand texture that an abstract summary would erase.

Use:

`participant label + safe role descriptor + short quote + one sentence of meaning`

Keep quotes brief, anonymized, and directly tied to the claim. Do not use a vivid quote to imply representativeness it does not have; state sample limits.

## Findings Versus Thinking

Separate evidence-backed findings from derived product or strategy thinking.

| Layer | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Conclusions / findings | Claims the supplied evidence can directly support. |
| Implications / thinking | Product routes, mechanisms, hypotheses, and next tests derived from the findings. |

If interviews do not prove a product route, label it as an implication or hypothesis. Preserve disagreement and counterexamples when they affect the decision.

## Conditional Evidence Hygiene

Evidence rules should protect the report's judgment, not define its personality. When a conclusion genuinely depends on a quantitative effect, causal comparison, current external fact, or durable operating assumption, keep the relevant scope and uncertainty visible and load `methodology.md` for the needed control. Otherwise do not add experimental design, threshold tables, claim ledgers, or operating contracts merely to make the report look rigorous.

## Language

State positive claims directly. Do not use “不是 A，而是 B” as a default rhetorical frame. Use contrast only when the distinction itself is decision-critical and evidenced.

Prefer integrated reasoning over slogan chains or marketplace-style bullet dumps. Explain the mechanism connecting evidence to the conclusion and the conclusion to the next decision.

## Final Check

- Does every conclusion answer the stated research question?
- Can each important claim be traced to a source or explicit unknown?
- Are sample limits and counterevidence visible?
- Are findings separated from derived product thinking?
- Does the report tell the reader what changes next?
- Can any section be removed without changing the decision? If yes, remove it.
