# Research Question Design

Use this reference when designing research questions, surveys, interviews, desk research, or a measurement plan. Start from the decision the research must improve. Do not begin by drafting questionnaire items.

## Start From The Decision

Write the decision in one sentence:

`After seeing the evidence, [decision owner] will choose / change [specific action] among [real alternatives].`

If no plausible answer would change a choice, scope, priority, or next test, the research is informational rather than decision-critical. Say so instead of manufacturing rigor.

## Build The Question Chain

Construct the chain in this order:

`core question -> subquestions -> competing hypotheses -> required information -> statistic or judgment -> decision use`

| Layer | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Core question | Name the uncertainty that blocks the decision, not a broad topic such as "understand users." |
| Subquestion | Isolate one cause, behavior, constraint, segment, mechanism, or alternative that could change the answer. |
| Competing hypotheses | State at least two plausible explanations or routes, including a disconfirming possibility. |
| Required information | Specify the observable behavior, comparison, account, document, trace, or measure needed to distinguish them. |
| Statistic or judgment | Define how the information will be interpreted before collecting it. Use a qualitative judgment when a statistic would be false precision. |
| Decision use | State exactly which option, scope, priority, or follow-up changes under each material result. |

Do not keep a subquestion merely because it is interesting. Remove it when its answer cannot affect the decision or interpretation of another decisive answer.

## Decompose The Core Question

Cover only dimensions that can change the choice. Useful candidates include:

- the current behavior and substitute, not only stated preference;
- the trigger and situation in which the problem becomes important;
- the actor who experiences the problem and the actor who decides or pays;
- frequency, severity, consequence, and workaround cost;
- constraints, permissions, trust, and reasons for non-action;
- differences between relevant groups or situations;
- evidence that would weaken the proposed explanation or solution.

Show dependency and priority when several questions are related. Do not present a flat list of equally important research themes.

## Keep Hypotheses Out Of The Respondent's Mouth

A hypothesis is a candidate explanation to test, not wording to plant in a question.

| Presupposition trap | Better route |
| --- | --- |
| "How useful is feature X?" | Ask what the person did in the last relevant situation, then test X separately. |
| "Why is X difficult?" | First establish whether, when, and for whom X is difficult; allow "not difficult." |
| "Which solution do you prefer?" | Include the current alternative, doing nothing, and relevant non-product routes. |
| "Would you pay for X?" | Ask about past spending, authority, budget source, tradeoff, and a concrete commitment test. |
| Positive-only reasons or scales | Include neutral, negative, unknown, and not-applicable paths. |

Do not report agreement with a prompted proposition as an independent discovery. Label it as reaction to the tested proposition.

## Derive Every Instrument Item Backward

Before keeping a survey or interview item, record:

| Item field | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Decision use | What changes if answers differ? |
| Target construct | Which behavior, constraint, belief, or outcome is being observed? |
| Answer form | What response can genuinely distinguish the hypotheses? |
| Interpretation | Which statistic, comparison, pattern, or qualitative judgment will be used? |
| Bias risk | What does the wording assume, signal, reward, or make hard to admit? |
| Branch | Who should see the item, skip it, or receive a follow-up? |

Ask about a specific recent episode before general attitudes. Use open discovery before presenting answer categories when those categories are still uncertain. Keep "unknown," "none," and "not applicable" when they are legitimate states.

## Require Independent Corroboration

For a finding that changes the recommendation, seek at least one meaningfully independent check:

- self-report against observed behavior, logs, transactions, documents, or actual choice;
- one stakeholder's account against another stakeholder with a different incentive or vantage point;
- stated demand against current workaround cost, commitment, adoption, repeat use, or payment behavior;
- an internal belief against external primary evidence or a fresh sample.

More respondents answering the same prompted item are replication, not independent corroboration. Two sources derived from the same underlying record are not independent. Preserve disagreement; investigate whether it reflects segment, situation, incentive, or measurement differences.

## Predefine Interpretation

- State the population, sample route, denominator, time window, and relevant segments.
- Separate prevalence from intensity and stated preference from observed behavior.
- Treat quotes as texture or mechanism evidence, not population estimates.
- Treat small or convenience samples as directional unless the sampling supports broader inference.
- Do not imply causality from correlation, recall, or before/after movement without a defensible comparison.
- Define the result that would weaken, narrow, or stop the favored hypothesis.

## Research Brief Output

Produce a compact map containing the decision, core question, prioritized subquestions, competing hypotheses, information sources, interpretation method, corroboration route, and result-to-decision branches. Then create only the interview guide, survey, search plan, or measurement artifact needed to fill that map.
