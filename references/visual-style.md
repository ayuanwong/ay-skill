# Visual Style

Choose visual language from content, audience, product state, brand assets, and use setting. AY does not have one universal house style.

## Direction Before Decoration

Before a substantial visual build or redesign, inspect:

- the real product/content and intended user;
- existing brand, screenshots, data, imagery, and prior artifact;
- device, venue, projection, mobile, or work-surface constraints;
- 2-3 plausible visual directions and the tradeoff between them.

Choose one direction and explain why it carries the content better. Do not default to an AI gradient, rounded-card SaaS dashboard, or sparse Apple imitation.

## Attention Rules

- Size, contrast, position, whitespace, and motion must follow decision priority.
- Use color for state, hierarchy, meaning, or identity—not random decoration.
- Use cards only for genuinely repeated units; tables, lists, split panes, diagrams, timelines, and documents may be better.
- Keep type scale and radii deliberate. If every block is large, bold, rounded, or colored, hierarchy has failed.
- Distinguish cognitive jobs: judgment, action, comparison, evidence, and detail should not all use the same component grammar.
- Give the main explanation enough space; do not trap it in a narrow rail because a familiar layout suggests one.
- Dense does not mean cramped. Sparse does not mean clear. Organize the amount of information the task actually needs.

## Product And Tool Surfaces

- Start with the user's first question and next action, not a hero section.
- Prefer quiet, fast, inspectable interaction for work tools.
- Use motion to explain state or causality; avoid decorative motion that slows work.
- Compress avoidable scrolling on desktop; preserve real depth when it exists.
- Verify responsive behavior, overflow, focus/selected state, empty/error state, and contrast.

## Audience Artifacts

Presentations, reports, event pages, and brand materials may need stronger identity and energy than a product dashboard.

- Use relevant photography, diagrams, posters, maps, screenshots, data, texture, or motion when they carry proof or identity.
- Do not use screenshot collages, fake device frames, icons, or decorative charts as substitute evidence.
- Technical proof can be restrained; brand/event/launch work may require higher density and stronger visual rhythm.
- Existing-artifact revisions must preserve or improve information, identity, live use, and editability.

## Artifact Proof

Source code, extracted text, and clean structure do not prove visual quality.

For a real page, app, or deck:

1. render or open it in the intended environment;
2. inspect the first viewport/page and the core path;
3. compare against the original when revising;
4. check mobile or projection conditions when relevant;
5. state any untested visual or interaction state.

A visually polished artifact that hides weak content, broken interaction, or unverified claims has failed.
